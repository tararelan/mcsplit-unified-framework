Note on down and rl down in the hard-instance heatmap. Down and rl down are top-down searches. They start with k = 0 unmatched vertices, which asks for the largest possible common subgraph, and raise k each time that size is shown to be infeasible. The other algorithms work bottom-up and improve their incumbent steadily as the search runs. On a hard instance, where no algorithm finishes, a bottom-up algorithm has therefore built up a sizeable incumbent when the timeout hits, whereas down is still working through the large sizes and has only reached a smaller incumbent, if any. This is why down and rl down almost never report a larger incumbent than another algorithm here, and why their columns are high. It reflects the search order, not the quality of the algorithms: on the instances that are solved, down and rl down solve the most (see the solved counts). Incumbent size at timeout therefore favours anytime algorithms and is not a fair measure of top-down ones.
